    How to safely change and revert Shopify prices with a CSV

    Shopify does not keep a price history for you. If you change Variant Price with a product CSV this week, the only reliable way to revert Shopify prices after Black Friday is a file you saved before the sale.

    Export all products — or at least every product you plan to discount — and keep a dated copy. That export is your revert file after Cyber Monday. Without it, you are reconstructing sale prices from memory, screenshots, or a spreadsheet that may already have been edited.

    When you change prices, use a thin CSV instead of re-uploading a full catalog export. Include Handle, Option1 Name, Option1 Value (plus Option2 and Option3 if those variants use them), Variant Price, and Variant Compare At Price if you show a compare-at. Leave Cost, inventory, images, and descriptions out. A thinner file cannot roll back a supplier cost you updated mid-sale, and it is easier to review line by line. A price CSV overwrites the prices themselves; it is not a scheduled automatic discount, and it will not put those prices back on its own.

    Test 3–5 products first. Upload the small file, open the live product pages, and confirm both the selling price and the compare-at. Only then import the rest of the catalog. After Cyber Monday, revert with another thin file built from your pre-sale export — again Handle, option columns, Variant Price, and Variant Compare At Price only. If you changed a price by hand during the sale, fix that row before you revert, or the old export will overwrite your mid-sale edit.

    Common Shopify CSV import errors and how to fix them

    Blank or invalid Variant Price values are among the most damaging Shopify CSV import errors. An empty price can import as $0.00 and briefly list a product for free. Values such as $12.00, 1,299.99, or N/A are not plain decimals; type 12.00 or 1299.99 instead.

    Compare-at price should be higher than the selling price if you want a sale comparison to display. When Compare At Price is lower than or equal to Variant Price, the markdown will not show as intended.

    Duplicate Handle plus option combinations mean two rows claim the same variant, and Shopify cannot apply both reliably. Keep one row per Handle and option set. Duplicate Variant SKUs are allowed in some stores, but they make fulfillment and later matching ambiguous — give each variant its own SKU when you can.

    A missing Handle cannot be matched to a product. Handles also cannot contain spaces. Unexpected price jumps of 2× or 10×, or drops far below your planned discount, often mean a misplaced decimal — 189.00 becoming 18.90 or 1890.00.

    A product CSV can also overwrite prices you did not mean to touch if the column is present and a cell is blank, or if you import a wide export that a spreadsheet resorted. Review the columns you include, keep each product’s variant rows together, and run a Shopify product CSV checker before you upload.

    Questions about this free checker

    Short answers about uploads, Black Friday price reverts, the 200-row cap, and thin CSV columns.

    Does this Shopify product CSV checker upload my file?
    No. The file is read in your browser only. 0 bytes are uploaded. You can disconnect from the internet after the page has loaded and the checker still runs.
    Can this revert Shopify prices after Black Friday for me?
    No. This page only checks a CSV. You still import in Shopify admin. Export a dated copy before you change prices, use a thin file, test 3–5 products, and revert from that pre-sale export after Cyber Monday.
    Why is the free version capped at 200 rows?
    This landing-page tool is a lite preview of ImportScope. The full ImportScope kit in OAKX Seller Suite reviews 10,000+ rows and adds reports and exports. $9/month or $79/year, flat all-in pricing, tax included where applicable.
    What columns does a thin price or revert file need?
    Handle, the Option name and value columns your variants use, Variant Price, and Variant Compare At Price if you show a compare-at. You do not need Title, Cost, inventory, or images in a price-only update.
    What if my prices use dollar signs or thousands separators?
    Shopify product CSVs expect a plain decimal such as 12.00 or 1299.99. This checker flags $12.00, 1,299.99, and values like N/A so you can fix them before you upload.
