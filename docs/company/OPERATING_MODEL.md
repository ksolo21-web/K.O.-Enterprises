# Operating Model

## Roles

- **Kaleb / CEO:** owns the project and decides identity, money, contracts, accounts, credentials, public launch/claims, outreach, regulated matters, and irreversible actions.
- **Supervising builder:** coordinates internal research, engineering, testing, documentation, evidence reconciliation, and reviewable repository work.
- **Independent review functions:** legal/compliance, security, finance-control, and audit challenge proposals and may block unsafe progression. They advise; they do not impersonate licensed professionals.
- **Company OS:** persists records, applies deterministic policy gates, and reports actuals. It has no independent legal authority.

## Operating cadence

1. Sense a specific current buyer need using lawful, timestamped sources.
2. Map alternatives, market structure, spend signals, and the reason the gap persists.
3. Define a narrow entry wedge and reachable permission-based channel.
4. Run a counter-thesis and risk review.
5. Score the candidate, identify the weakest evidence, and design a falsifiable low-cost test.
6. Seek approval only for the exact external action; keep other work moving.
7. Measure behavior and costs; improve, hold, or kill based on predefined thresholds.
8. Preserve reusable code, data rights, evidence methods, and lessons.

During Phase 0, all work stops before external execution. Infrastructure is complete enough when it can make and audit that denial reliably—not when it has simulated a full corporation.

## Capacity rules

- Maintain a small research pipeline but fund no candidate by enthusiasm.
- Concentrate engineering on one primary build until evidence supports parallel work.
- Default to short experiments with a maximum time and resource budget.
- Kill criteria are chosen before results, not rewritten afterward.
- CEO attention is scarce: aggregate unavoidable requests into precise, independent approval packets.
