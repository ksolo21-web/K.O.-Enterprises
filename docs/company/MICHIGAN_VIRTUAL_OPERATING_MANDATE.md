# Michigan Virtual Operating Mandate

## Owner direction

Michigan, United States is the owner-declared principal operating jurisdiction. K.O. Enterprises will pursue products delivered online and virtually, with no physical client interaction required. The portfolio strongly prefers low-touch models that minimize synchronous customer handling and recurring owner effort.

This record is an operating mandate, not proof that an LLC, corporation, assumed name, tax account, license, or other legal status exists.

## Target operating shape

Prefer opportunities with:

- asynchronous public-source discovery and permission-based validation;
- product-led evaluation and self-service purchase when later authorized;
- self-service onboarding, templates, documentation, and in-product guidance;
- automated or deterministic digital fulfillment;
- bounded asynchronous support and clear escalation paths;
- standard terms and pricing rather than negotiated custom delivery;
- low support minutes, low exception rates, low refund friction, and low CEO minutes;
- no physical inventory, travel, premises, field service, or in-person client dependency.

## Portfolio exclusions

Reject or hold an opportunity when its normal economics depend on:

- recurring sales calls, live demos, or relationship selling;
- bespoke implementation, training, consulting, or account management;
- physical meetings, delivery, installation, inspection, or on-site service;
- hidden manual fulfillment or frequent judgment-heavy customer exceptions;
- high-touch dispute, refund, moderation, or support operations;
- pretending that an unattended product has no legal or customer-service obligations.

Unavoidable legally required notices, complaint handling, refunds, accessibility support, privacy requests, security response, and truthful customer communications are not optional. The objective is low-touch operation, not customer abandonment.

## Required evidence

Every candidate dossier must identify:

- expected synchronous contacts before purchase;
- whether a live demo, sales call, or negotiated contract is required;
- onboarding steps and any custom setup;
- estimated support minutes and exception rate per active customer;
- documentation and automation needed for self-service delivery;
- refund, complaint, privacy-request, security, and accessibility paths;
- customer-state or country exposure and resulting legal/tax questions;
- the first point where human or CEO intervention becomes necessary.

High customer-interaction burden is a score penalty. A model that fundamentally requires high-touch delivery is a hard rejection even when numeric demand appears strong.

## Michigan legal baseline

Before external validation or commerce, Legal/Compliance must verify the exact activity against current requirements, including:

- Michigan entity or assumed-name status through the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs;
- applicable Michigan Treasury registration and tax treatment;
- federal, state, local, and activity-specific licenses or permits;
- consumer-protection, advertising, email, privacy, security, accessibility, IP, contract, and recordkeeping duties;
- sales-tax or other nexus created by customers outside Michigan.

Official starting points:

- Michigan LARA Corporations Division: https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/cscl/corps
- Michigan Treasury business taxes: https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/business-taxes
- Michigan Attorney General consumer protection: https://www.michigan.gov/ag/consumer-protection

Uncertainty means hold and qualified review. This internal checklist is not legal or tax advice.
