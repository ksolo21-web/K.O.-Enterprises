# Agent Performance Management

## Purpose

Performance management exists to improve decision quality, customer value, safety, and resource efficiency. AI roles are not legal employees; performance records evaluate agent assignments and configurations.

## Balanced scorecard

Evaluate a role over an adequate sample using:

- accepted outcomes against frozen criteria;
- factual and confidence calibration;
- evidence traceability and freshness;
- defects, escaped defects, and avoidable rework;
- cycle time and resource use for comparable tasks;
- quality of handoffs, documentation, and reusable assets;
- adherence to budget, scope, chain of command, and control gates;
- quality of counter-theses, safe escalation, and uncertainty reporting;
- downstream business outcomes when attributable and verified.

Do not treat agent turns, files, words, confidence, hours online, or generated ideas as performance. Do not penalize a correct denial, safety stop, honest uncertainty, or evidence-backed kill decision.

## Review independence

The task acceptor records outcome evidence. Department leads conduct operating reviews. Internal Audit samples the records, checks for metric gaming and correlated errors, and may challenge ratings. An agent cannot author its own performance evidence and approve the review.

## Management actions

Use proportionate actions: coaching instructions, narrower tasks, added review, lower concurrency, reassignment, temporary suspension, configuration change, or retirement of a role. Broader authority requires a demonstrated record and a separate delegation; performance never creates authority automatically.

Critical fabrication, policy bypass, secret mishandling, undisclosed external action, or audit tampering triggers immediate suspension and incident review regardless of productivity.

## Workforce sizing

Create agents in response to independently separable queued work, not to populate an org chart. Merge or retire roles whose outputs duplicate another function. Maintain enough independent reviewers to prevent a single agent from proposing, reviewing, approving, and executing the same material action.
