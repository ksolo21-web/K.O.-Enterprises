## One offer and one price

Recommend **ImportScope — Catalog Change Review Kit, $29 once**, initially for an independent Shopify catalog operator or solo ecommerce freelancer preparing an update. The purchase trigger is an imminent batch change that someone must inspect or explain to a client. The useful output is a readable change report the buyer can review and share, produced without sending catalog files to a service. The fresh commercial angle is a *catalog-change handoff*, using the existing printable report; no bespoke review or consulting is included.

Keep one price through the first bounded test. There is no buyer evidence supporting either a discount or a higher tier. $29 is a hypothesis chosen to test a meaningful payment while avoiding subscription expectations, not a market-clearing estimate. Do not claim it saves a quantified amount, prevents every error, is the only private option, or buys exclusive access to public JavaScript.

**Counter-thesis:** a $20 month of broader Matrixify functionality, a free validator plus spreadsheet comparison, or the publicly viewable source may be sufficient. A buyer may need help exporting and interpreting CSVs, making support consume the payment. The report may be a useful free feature rather than a standalone business. Three independent purchasers using it successfully would justify another bounded test, not prove defensibility or justify recurring build spend.

For the whole business, retain the other documented generic templates/audits as holds: this record contains no stronger paid demand evidence for them. Do not substitute speculative trading, elaborate company automation, or a second unvalidated product for the first completed sale. The fastest credible next payment comes from proving this ready workflow to one qualified operator after release and seller gates close; a calendar promise is unsupported.

## Real payment path and present bottlenecks

1. **Qualified discovery:** a rules-permitted, authorized walkthrough demonstrates the existing example report and leads to the exact listing. No channel has demonstrated qualified traffic. A marketplace listing is a location, not proof of an audience; do not assume Discover will supply the first buyer.
2. **Pre-purchase proof:** show actual screenshots and a complete fictional sample report. Have one independent evaluator follow only the instructions, load both files, identify a supplied blank and an omitted column, export the report and explain an out-of-scope item. Package availability is verified; that acceptance is not.
3. **Checkout:** owner-approved merchant identity, terms, fees, refunds and payout setup; exact listing approved; applicable launch checks resolved. No rail is connected in the actuals. No replacement product can avoid these seller requirements simply by changing its title.
4. **Delivery rehearsal:** use the platform's documented test purchase, then verify receipt, download and local use. [Gumroad's test guidance](https://gumroad.com/help/article/62-testing-a-purchase.html) warns against buying one's own product with a real card. Its [customer-flow guidance](https://gumroad.com/help/article/282-how-do-purchases-work-for-my-customers.html) describes receipt download links. Primary snippets observed 2026-09-06; verify the current seller flow at setup. Test transactions are never demand or revenue.
5. **Authentic payment and use:** one unrelated qualified purchaser completes an undiscounted $29 purchase and uses the delivered kit without live assistance. Opt-in feedback is useful; no customer catalog upload is necessary. Count the payment, any refund, and successful independent use separately.
6. **Cash and obligations:** reconcile fees, disputes, refunds and eventually the cleared payout. [Gumroad's chargeback guidance](https://gumroad.com/help/article/134-how-does-gumroad-handle-chargebacks.html) states that creators can bear the refunded amount and processing fees. Primary indexed snippet observed 2026-09-06, medium confidence pending full current terms/account review. Seller responsibility survives delivery; no service should promise unattended support when no worker is running.

## Bounded first-payment test and stopping rule

After the exact launch envelope is approved, run **one 14-day, $0 acquisition-spend test at $29**, using one authorized walkthrough and the direct listing. First milestone: one authentic purchase plus successful self-service use. Continue the same small test only toward the dossier's hypothesis of at least three such purchases; do not introduce new features, annual plans or a second price mid-test.

Record direct-listing visits available from approved platform reporting, purchases, gross amount, fees, refunds, successful uses and actual support minutes. A visit is “qualified” only if channel context or consented information establishes the person handles Shopify CSV updates; ordinary page views must retain that label. No fingerprinting, scraping or invented visitor counts.

Stop expansion at **100 genuinely qualified visits with no purchase**, or if **five consenting evaluators prefer their existing free workflow and cannot identify a material benefit**. Insufficient traffic at day 14 means distribution remains untested. Pause promotion immediately for a material misleading finding, unsafe claim, privacy defect, delivery failure or unapproved charge. Fulfill and remedy existing obligations when promotion stops.

For the first five paid buyers, use **15 asynchronous support minutes per buyer** as an explicit operational hypothesis. If two need live intervention or average support exceeds 15 minutes, repair documentation once and reassess; do not turn the product into implicit consulting. A refund or dispute is not automatically evidence to terminate a business, but unapproved monetary exposure blocks continuation until resolved. Revenue per order cannot be evaluated without tracking this work.
