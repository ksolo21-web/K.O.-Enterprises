## Buyer and business cases

**Publisher puzzle.** Buyer and budget holder: a small publisher's owner or audience lead. Proposed job: add a repeat daily engagement habit. Workarounds include existing games, free plugins and doing nothing. Lost retention/ad income is a proposed cost of inaction, not a measured loss. Current publisher use and supplier prices support a category, while free substitutes weaken the generic offer [E1–E3]. The strongest objection is that another game adds no measurable value worth paying for. Reopen only for a current, specific publisher requirement unmet by suitable alternatives and credible buying intent.

**Procurement alerts.** Buyer: a small supplier's owner or bid lead. Job: identify suitable opportunities and material changes. Workarounds include official portals, email alerts, paid research tools and staff. A missed bid can matter, but no attributable loss or underserved segment was verified. The observed job request includes substantial human proposal work; it is not a software order [E4–E5]. Reject generic aggregation without evidence of a precise, economically important coverage gap that can be served reliably at $0 cash cost.

**Stocky history archive.** Buyer: a small retailer's owner or purchasing manager who still needs old purchasing records. Proposed outcome: search authorized exports by PO, SKU and date in a local tool. Existing alternatives include keeping CSVs, spreadsheets and replacement inventory apps. A provider-confirmed native limitation creates a plausible gap, not proof that a separate paid archive is needed [E6]. Vendor offerings, a free checker and a merchant replacement review show supply response [E7–E9]. Export completeness and support effort remain untested. Never promise recovery of missing data or restoration of historical orders into an unsupported native system.

## Profitability and upside

No defensible revenue forecast can be calculated yet. The unknown inputs are qualified reachable buyers, conversion, price acceptance, repeat usage, support time and refund/dispute exposure. Do not multiply all Shopify stores or all publishers by an arbitrary price and call it an addressable market.

For any future test, calculate collected sales less processing/platform fees, delivery, attributable acquisition and support costs, and expected refunds/disputes. Show fixed costs, taxes and owner time separately. A local file-based design may avoid per-use server charges; it does not make acquisition, maintenance or error handling free. A single platform sunset is a temporary sales window unless a repeatable adjacent need is evidenced. No merchant terms or payment configuration were changed.

## Completed archive qualification and stop conditions

Completed a focused follow-through within a **two-internal-hour ceiling**, using four additional targeted searches, the available export discussion and the linked replacement listing. Sought at least three distinct current merchant reports of an unresolved history-access problem and a credible budget/commitment signal. This threshold was fixed before follow-through. It was not met. The public evidence instead included vendor solicitation, changing technical claims and merchants reporting successful use of a free alternative [E10–E11]. The absence of a qualifying buyer set in this bounded search does not establish zero market demand.

**Close the generic archive lead as insufficiently differentiated and stop its build.** No outreach or product implementation was needed to reach that decision. A privacy-sensitive offline segment could differ, but no buyer evidence supports adding that requirement now. Reopen only on a new, verifiable unmet requirement with buying intent; do not relabel this same idea to evade the failed screen.

For the next distinct candidate, start with current buyer requests for a costly recurring workflow. The next deliverable is a deduplicated buyer-evidence packet, not another prototype. Qualify the problem, suitable-supply gap, lawful inputs and feasible free acquisition route before an offer/example. At most three candidates per screen; return no winner if none qualifies. A screening threshold is not statistical proof of high demand.

Any later buyer test must name an exact scope and price hypothesis, qualified-exposure denominator, a fixed time limit, support ceiling and success/kill criteria before results. A missing audience or checkout means it did not start. The first genuine paid delivery is the next commercial evidence milestone, not a guarantee of scale.
