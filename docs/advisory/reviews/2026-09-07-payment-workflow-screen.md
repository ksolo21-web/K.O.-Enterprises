## Specific buyer record and qualification questions

**Record CON-001:** original poster in S4; observed 2026-09-07. User: project manager at a self-described small glazing shop. Budget holder: unverified. Existing paid-tool use: QuickBooks reported; actual expenditure unknown. Desired output: project-level contract value, invoiced totals and remaining billing in one view. Status: no contact, no commitment, no K.O. customer.

The same buyer later expresses reluctance to maintain another spreadsheet and considers a connected custom app. Therefore zero-server CSV processing may save our costs while adding their work. That is a material product-fit risk. A vendor reply says its QuickBooks reporting tool can already produce the requested report; the claim is not a tested implementation.

For any future authorized buyer conversation, resolve the actual gap rather than pitch an invented feature:

1. Is the need still unresolved, and which available report/product was tried?
2. Which decision cannot be made from the current tools, and what happened the last time it was delayed?
3. What exact fields, accounting edition and refresh frequency are required?
4. Is automatic synchronization mandatory, or is an export acceptable in practice?
5. What is already spent on the task, and who can approve a purchase?
6. What measurable result would make a paid, limited trial worthwhile?

This is an unsent discovery guide. No message was posted and no private data requested. Do not infer posting or customer-contact authority from finding a public thread.

## Profit sensitivity: scenario only

To avoid treating unpaid owner work as profit, consider an explicitly hypothetical $49 recurring offer. Assume $5 per customer-period for non-labor variable costs and value time at $30/hour. These are modeling inputs, not our selected price, Stripe fee schedule or measured costs.

| Illustrative workload in a customer-period | Contribution before fixed costs and tax |
| --- | --- |
| 15 minutes of support; no acquisition effort allocated | $36.50 |
| 15 minutes of support plus two hours of acquisition effort | -$23.50 |

Calculation: price minus non-labor variable costs minus time multiplied by the hourly value. Acquisition cost allocation across later periods would require observed retention. Excluded fixed development, tax and any costs exceeding the placeholder must remain visible. No forecast follows from these examples; they show why cheap software alone does not establish profit or autonomy.
