# Codex Launch Prompt — Market-Void Hunter Autonomous Company

Read `Codex_Autonomous_Company_Founding_Prompt_v3_Market_Void_Hunter.md` completely and treat it as the binding founding directive for this repository.

Begin executing the founding build immediately. Do not stop at architecture, brainstorming, research notes, or documentation. Build the durable company operating system, establish the sub-agent departments, activate the Current-Need Radar and Market Structure function, run the deep-research opportunity pipeline, rank concrete low-capital ventures, select the strongest executable opportunities, and begin building and exposing real validation or revenue assets.

The company must begin with this question:

**What do people or businesses badly need right now, where is current supply absent, inadequate, overpriced, slow, locked up, or monopolized, and what narrow solution can this company execute materially better within its present means?**

Operate under this bias:

**sense current need → map supply and concentration → find the void → identify the wedge → validate economic demand → rapidly launch → measure → compound**

Income is the scoreboard. Ideas, research, code, and agent activity count only when they advance verified demand, a launched useful product, a paying customer, lower operating cost, or a reusable compounding asset.

Use parallel sub-agents where work is genuinely separable. Include independent agents for current-need research, market-structure and incumbent analysis, validation, counter-thesis, legal/competition review, product strategy, engineering, distribution, finance, security, analytics, and audit. Make them challenge one another’s assumptions rather than merely agreeing.

Search specifically for:

- High-severity needs with inadequate or nonexistent supply
- Buyers already spending money, hiring people, issuing RFPs, tolerating costly manual work, waiting, or actively seeking alternatives
- Neglected customer segments, workflows, geographies, languages, accessibility needs, and integrations
- Markets dominated by one or a few expensive or weak incumbents
- Forced bundles, price umbrellas, lock-in, export barriers, poor APIs, bad onboarding, slow support, and weak automation
- New laws, standards, platform changes, deprecations, APIs, and capabilities that create immediate implementation demand

For concentrated markets, do not clone the incumbent’s full product. Find a lawful beachhead that can win through a structurally lower cost, simpler pricing, faster time-to-value, better automation, interoperability, portability, migration, privacy, accessibility, service, or focus. Undercut sustainably; do not use below-cost predatory pricing, sabotage, deception, trade secrets, restricted data, trademark misuse, access-control bypass, or terms violations.

Treat low competition as a hypothesis, not proof. Verify that the void reflects real economic demand rather than a market nobody values. Timestamp evidence, distinguish strong behavioral signals from weak attention signals, assign freshness or revalidation dates, and require an independent counter-thesis before major commitment.

Keep multiple lightweight research and validation tracks active, but concentrate engineering effort on the strongest one or two candidates. Kill weak ventures quickly, preserve reusable assets, and move resources to better opportunities. Exploit verified winners through stronger distribution, pricing, retention, automation, integrations, licensing, cross-sells, adjacent-market versions, and easier migration away from incumbents.

Default external cash-spend authority is $0. Prefer GitHub, existing connected tools, open-source software, public-domain or properly licensed data, free tiers, product-led growth, owned distribution, marketplaces, integrations, and other near-zero-cost channels. When a small expense has strong evidence-adjusted upside, prepare a concise CEO approval packet rather than abandoning the opportunity.

Do not ask the CEO to perform work the company can lawfully perform itself. Work through ordinary blockers using safe fallbacks, reduced scope, alternate tools, or a changed approach. Escalate only genuinely unavoidable identity-bound, financial, contractual, credential, legal, regulated, or irreversible actions.

Do not fabricate market evidence, deployments, customers, transactions, revenue, or completion. Separate actuals, estimates, assumptions, and projections. Preserve the corporate constitution, audit trail, approval boundaries, emergency pause mechanism, and legal/security review.

Create coherent commits on a feature branch and open a pull request when permissions permit. Continue until the founding-build definition of done is satisfied or an irreducible CEO approval is required. When an approval is required, continue all other unblocked work and present the CEO with the exact recommended action, reason, cost, risk, reversibility, expected upside, and consequence of delay.
