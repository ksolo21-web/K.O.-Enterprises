**Capture date:** 2026-11-05  
**Proposal ID:** KO-IDEA-003  
**Status:** SAVED FOR FUTURE VALIDATION — NOT AN ACTIVE THIRD LAUNCH  
**Business type:** B2B service matchmaking / buyer-paid vendor sourcing / commercial-service introductions  
**Owner-directed active priorities remain:** [Market Intelligence + Contractor Operations](../market-intelligence/BUILD_PLAN.md)  
**Operating boundary:** `PAUSE_AUTONOMY` active; no unsolicited outreach, external spend, commitments, automatic referrals, or sharing customer data.

## Exact proposed customer problem
A company needs another business to perform a specialized service, but finding available, suitably qualified, compatible local/regional providers and comparing reliable information consumes time. Conversely, qualified service providers need customers and viable partner relationships.

**Business concept:** K.O. would document the buyer's requested scope, search lawful public and buyer-authorized sources, independently verify claims to the degree possible, prepare a transparent shortlist of candidate providers, obtain opt-in introductions after approval, and document the results. Buyer and provider contract directly. Avoid acting as the general contractor, negotiating regulated transactions, controlling client funds, guaranteeing provider performance, or assuming obligations of either party.

The market also includes complementary strategic B2B referrals (e.g. a contractor needing an authorized specialist), but this does not itself justify establishing a generic business marketplace.

## Focused submarkets worth investigating (hypotheses, not validated rankings)

A. **Overflow/backup commercial-service sourcing** for regional facility managers or commercial contractors with intermittent gaps in HVAC, janitorial, floor care, pest control, commercial glass, door access or specialist service. Focus on **small portfolios / under 20 or under 50 locations** that might not benefit from enterprise vendor management stacks. The suggested segment boundary is hypothetical.

B. **Subcontractor-to-subcontractor commercial introductions** for capacity shortfalls, after confirming licensing, contracting and site safety requirements; avoid regulated residential builder solicitation/referral structures.

C. **Vendor qualification and comparison as a service**, where a buyer pays for a documented shortlist with verification-date, license/insurance *evidence status*, capability fit, uncertainty, transparent fee and alternatives. More defensible than a hidden commission-driven introduction.

D. **Specialized complementary business partnerships** (e.g. IT managed services paired with authorized cybersecurity assessor), but sales cycles, conflicts, qualification and competition remain untested.

**Initial priority for a later small test:** A plus C in Southeast Michigan commercial services, using the industry knowledge from the two active businesses. Avoid creating a large marketplace, two-sided mobile app or paid advertising before observing buyer willingness to pay.

## Potential revenue structure — not market prices

- Preferred conflict-minimizing model: **buyer-paid fixed research/sourcing fee**, with a fully disclosed scope, decision ownership and refund/dispute terms. Sample test price concept: $199–$499 for a limited vendor shortlist **only if** workload and buyer interest support it.
- Alternative: vendor-paid **disclosed** introduction fee under written, lawful arrangements. **Do not implement** percentages of projects or closed-contract commissions until counsel reviews category-specific licensing and fee rules.
- Avoid undisclosed double-sided payments, pay-to-rank without disclosure, fake neutrality and guaranteed-provider representations.

## Regulatory, reputation and independence risk — critical

- Michigan LARA says **anyone who, for compensation, solicits or secures business for a residential builder or maintenance-and-alteration contractor must be licensed** to that builder/contractor as a salesperson, including certain referral-fee situations. No unlicensed residential builder referral launch. https://www.michigan.gov/lara/bureau-list/bcc/sections/licensing-section/residential-builders/lic-info/salesperson-license-information
- Mortgage settlement-service referral fees face separate federal restrictions; Michigan DIFS describes Regulation X's prohibition. No mortgage/insurance/real estate/finance, government procurement kickbacks or other regulated fee arrangements without appropriate legal review. https://www.michigan.gov/difs/news-and-outreach/faq/consumer-finance/mortgage-compliance2
- FTC guidance expects clear disclosure of paid relationships when they could affect recommendations. Be explicit about who pays, compensation structure, conflicts and ranking criteria. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
- **Market Intelligence impartiality risk:** A service provider paying for introductions may bias supposedly objective client recommendations. Maintain separate service terms, fee disclosures and client data permissions; never represent a paid placement as independent market research.
- Do not claim a contractor is licensed/insured/currently available from an unverified webpage. Record licensing authority and check date, insurer/COI evidence and caveats. Verification is never a guarantee of workmanship or contract performance.
- Buyer consent and confidentiality are mandatory before forwarding requests. Final matching decisions and direct vendor contracts belong to the two businesses. Do not direct a client's choices, promise outcomes, or mislead through vague “could be” wording.

## Research and decision gates before activation

1. **Buyer discovery:** identify 20 plausible small commercial-service buyers, then obtain 8–10 real, authorized conversations about recent vendor-search failures, size of need, time lost and budget; no fabricated demand.
2. **Competitive gap:** compare at least 5 alternatives (search, incumbent providers, paid directories/platforms, procurement desks, internal staff), local coverage and pricing; verify any unserved claim.
3. **Supply validation:** recruit no vendors until permitted. Later check at least 10 potential specialty vendors with licensing, insurance, capacity and geographic coverage using approved methods; no guarantee.
4. **Paid signal:** aim for at least 2 explicit paid pilot commitments for a transparent, scoped vendor shortlist; recorded payments/agreements required to count as validation.
5. **Economics and safety:** measure actual time, sourcing cost, conflicts, complaints/qualification errors, privacy and legal review. If no willingness to pay or high regulation, remain parked.
6. **Critic:** all client deliverables must pass the shared 12-point evidence/document critic gate, strictly >9/10 independent review plus all mandatory checks, then exact approved release control. Root autonomy pause continues.
